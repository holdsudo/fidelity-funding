How can my trucking company improve approval odds for a merchant cash advance?

Fidelity FundingNov 26, 2025· 3 min read

Merchant cash advances (MCAs) can be a fast way for trucking companies to access working capital, but they also come with strict underwriting criteria and high effective costs. Improving your approval odds means presenting clean, predictable cash flow and organized documentation that underwriters can verify quickly. This article breaks down the specific steps trucking businesses should take to maximize their chances of MCA approval.

What This Means for Trucking Companies

For trucking companies, MCA underwriters focus less on credit score and more on the predictability of cash inflows — including fuel card receipts, freight broker payments, and factoring deposits. Because the industry often works with variable loads, brokers, and net-30 terms, lenders will look for steady deposits, long-term broker relationships, and documentation that ties revenue to real freight activity (BOLs, rate confirmations, and invoices). Improving approval odds often involves cleaning up banking and merchant processing history, separating personal and business finances, and being able to show reliable cash collections over time. Small changes to payments processes and record-keeping can make the difference between a decline and an approval with favorable terms.

Key Factors to Understand

  • Cash flow consistency: Underwriters prioritize regular, verifiable deposits (daily or weekly) over occasional big months; consistency signals lower default risk.
  • Document trail: Brokers contracts, rate confirmations, BOLs, invoices, and fleet documentation (DOT authority, insurance) give context to deposits and reduce perceived risk.
  • Payment processing type: ACH and factoring deposits are viewed differently than credit card receipts; some MCA products require merchant processing statements while others accept bank statements or factoring statements.
  • Time in business and revenue history: Most funders want at least 3–6 months of stable history; longer histories and upward revenue trends improve odds and pricing.
  • Operational risks: Chargebacks, frequent NSF, high expense volatility (fuel spikes), and heavy owner draws can lower approval chances.

Practical Steps You Can Take

  • Organize and standardize financial records: Gather 3–12 months of business bank statements, merchant processing statements (if used), factoring statements, and accounts receivable records. Label deposits to match BOLs, invoices, or rate confirmations so underwriters can trace cash back to freight.
  • Separate business and personal finances: Move all trucking income and expenses to a dedicated business account. Consistent, clearly business-only deposits reduce questions and speed underwriting.
  • Stabilize deposit patterns: Where possible, shift to predictable payment timing — encourage brokers to use direct deposit on set days, standardize billing terms, or consider a factoring relationship that provides steady daily deposits.
  • Compile operational documentation: Have copies of DOT authority, active insurance, vehicle titles/leases, driver lists, broker contracts/rate confirmations, and proof of on-time deliveries (BOLs). A transparent paper trail lowers risk and builds trust.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mingling personal and business transactions — this obscures true cash flow and raises red flags during underwriting.
  • Handing over incomplete or unlabeled statements — unorganized records slow approvals and can lead to denials if underwriters can’t verify revenue sources.
  • Relying only on cash transactions — heavy cash deposits without supporting documentation make it harder to prove business viability.
  • Waiting until you need funding to clean records — funders prefer seeing consistent, deliberate financial practices; last-minute fixes rarely sway underwriting.

Final Thoughts

Trucking companies can significantly improve MCA approval odds by making their cash flow transparent and predictable, organizing documentation that ties deposits to freight, and reducing operational red flags like mixed accounts or frequent NSF items. Small process changes — separating accounts, standardizing billing, and compiling broker agreements and BOLs — often produce quicker approvals and better terms than chasing higher credit scores.
